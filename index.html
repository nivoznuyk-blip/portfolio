More than screens

Since 2024 I’ve worked at two mobile startups as both designer and product analyst: market and competitor research, product logic settled before the first mockup. I start with “why does the user need this”, not “how do I make it pretty”.

From sourcing to the product page

I owned and ran a brick-and-mortar retail store as a sole proprietor: supplier sourcing, assortment, merchandising, marketing and a team of three. I made the product pages and ads myself — so I know what has to be in the frame for the product to actually sell.

Prompt engineering and automation

Writing, testing and tuning prompts for a specific job. Building pipelines in n8n and Make and working directly with the OpenAI and Anthropic APIs.

A lookbook with no studio

A full apparel line shot with no studio, no models and no photographer. One backdrop, one lighting setup, one framing logic — full-length, fit detail, back view — ready to drop into a product page.

The same system in motion: model, turn, fabric close-up, walk. Two clips — minimal streetwear and a premium line indoors — share the same shot logic with a different light and pace. Built for marketplace listings and social feeds.

These clips still carry the generation service’s watermark — clean versions are available on request.

Illustrated storytelling

Four narrative pieces, from 18 seconds to over a minute: first-person ink horror, an ancient saga, a Christmas story and a city chronicle. Each holds its own illustrated style from the first frame to the last. At this length it stops being a generation problem and becomes a storytelling one: the palette can’t drift between scenes, and the pacing has to keep people watching to the end.

FormCheck carousel

An educational carousel for a fitness account: eight slides on close-grip bench press form. The same character and the same gym in every frame — only the pose and the focus change. On top sits a custom layout system: brand tag, series label, highlighted headline, on-photo callouts and a closing CTA. The script is built to be swiped to the end: mistake, recognition, two fixes, result, a plan you can use.

One character, six locations

A study in consistency: the same woman across six locations — Tokyo neon, snowfall, a stone alley, a street market, headlights on a bridge and a close-up portrait. Face, coat and light quality hold from frame to frame — that is what turns a set of images into a campaign.

Brand videos

Three videos in the language of specific brands: furniture unboxing in eight seconds, a 25-second mascot story, and retail. Each one is made the way the brand would make it — its tone, its palette, its product on screen.

Texture, light, era

Product shots for skincare and fragrance — hard shadow on plaster, green onyx, wet stone. Plus a separate track: recreating 1960s archive photography, where it reads as real because of film grain, fluorescent light and smoke under the ceiling.

A still image brought to motion: smoke rises, a lamp flickers, people shift their weight. Useful for museum, documentary and retro projects.

Websites and landing pages

Two designs: a movie news portal and an online store. Different goals and different visual languages, the same approach — screen logic and the user’s path first, decoration second.

Marquee — a box office news portal

Six screens, a design system and a responsive layout down to 390 px. The visual language borrows from 1930s–50s American movie palaces — brass, oxblood, marquee bulbs — but the numbers come first: box office totals lead every screen, and the decoration stays in the frame.

Podyom — a balloon gift box store

An online store for surprise boxes with helium balloons, delivered across Minsk within two hours, around the clock. The whole design serves one goal: get people to checkout while they’re still in the mood. Ordering takes one screen and seven fields, no account required, with confirmation sent by messenger.
